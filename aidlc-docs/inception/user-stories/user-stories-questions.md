# User Stories — Story Plan and Questions

> Based on `requirements.md` (FR-1 to FR-10, NFR-1 to NFR-12). Stories will follow the
> INVEST criteria (independent, negotiable, valuable, estimable, small, testable), carry
> a MoSCoW priority taken from the requirements, and have Given/When/Then acceptance
> criteria. Three choices shape the set.

## Q1. How should the user be described?
There is one user, but they act in different modes.

- A. **One persona** — the owner, with their goals and pain points described once.
- B. **One person, three modes** — Maker (capturing parts to design around), Explorer (studying the hardware), Checker (establishing accuracy). Each story names the mode.
- X. Other (please specify)

[Answer]: B

## Q2. How should stories be grouped?
- A. **By workflow** — the three flows from the mockups (scan a part and use it; find out what to trust; see the hardware), plus a foundation group for delivery and diagnostics.
- B. **By capability** — one group per requirement area (FR-1 to FR-10).
- C. **By delivery order** — grouped in the risk-first sequence of the backlog.
- X. Other (please specify)

[Answer]: A

## Q3. How fine-grained should stories be?
- A. **Coarse** — about 12–15 stories, each a whole capability you can try end to end on the phone.
- B. **Medium** — about 20–25 stories, splitting capture, guidance, interruption and export paths apart.
- C. **Fine** — about 40 or more, close to one story per requirement.
- X. Other (please specify)

[Answer]: A
